# Emerging Fields of Public Administration — Group 4

An interactive 82-slide presentation and study deck on the emerging fields of Public Administration, prepared as a group report for PCU.

**Open the presentation:** https://tenrenti01.github.io/PCUemergingfieldsofpublicadministration/

## What is inside

| File | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `index.html` | The presentation. One self-contained file — no internet or install needed. |
| `Group4-Presentation-and-Speech.pdf` | All 82 slides as pictures, each with the speech the presenter reads aloud. |

## Topics covered

1. **Introduction** — what public administration is, where it came from (the spoils system, the Pendleton Act of 1883, Woodrow Wilson), and how thinking about government changed over time.
2. **Part A · Public Enterprise Management** — what a GOCC is, why governments create them, the boom and bust of 1965–1984, privatization, and the Governance Commission for GOCCs created by RA 10149 after the 2010 MWSS bonus scandal.
3. **Part B · Voluntary Sector Management** — pluralist theory and its six principles, and the roles of civil society, NGOs and volunteer groups, from bayanihan to EDSA, the 1987 Constitution and the PDAF scam.
4. **Part C · Technology and Digital Governance** — data and IT in the public sector, e-government and its four stages, the Philippine story from RA 8792 to eGovPH, and inter-sectoral and intergovernmental networks.
5. **Wrap-up** — a one-table recap, a quick quiz, discussion questions and the sources.

## How to present

Click **Present** in the bar at the bottom (or press **P**) to open presenter view:

- **Left column** — every slide stacked vertically, with the current one centered.
- **Top right** — the current slide beside a peek of the next slide.
- **Bottom right** — the speech to read aloud, in large text.
- All the dividers can be dragged, and double-clicking one resets it.

Click **Audience screen** to open a second window that shows only the slide. Drag that window to the projector and click inside it once for full screen — it follows your slides automatically.
